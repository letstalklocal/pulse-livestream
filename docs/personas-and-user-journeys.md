# Pulse VIP personas and user journeys

These three personas describe who Pulse VIP serves and what each person is trying to accomplish. Use them when comparing feature ideas, prioritizing work, and checking whether a flow solves a real user problem. They are product decision guides, not a claim that every capability below exists or has been approved for implementation. Feature-specific decisions and current status remain in the relevant product documents.

## Valentina, the growing creator

**Profile:** Valentina is 24, lives in Colombia, and is a part-time creator and social media personality. She uses TikTok, Instagram, and live-streaming apps heavily on her phone, but is not technical.

**Main goal:** Turn her audience and personality into reliable income while building a loyal group of followers.

**Motivations:** Earn through gifts and premium content, bring regular viewers back, grow her following, and eventually make streaming a significant income source.

**Typical behavior:** She goes live several nights a week, chats actively, remembers regulars, and encourages gifts without asking constantly. She promotes streams on social media and may invite existing followers to Pulse VIP.

**Pain points:** An empty room at the start of a stream, weak discovery, viewers leaving for other creators, unclear earnings or payouts, trolls, and the possibility that substantial streaming time will not pay off.

**What she needs:** Strong discovery; simple Go Live controls; clear earnings, diamonds, and cash-out information; follower notifications; moderation; viewer insights; ways to identify VIPs and regulars; exciting gifts; premium or private experiences; and ways to recognize and reward loyal supporters.

**Product lens:** Pulse VIP is an audience and income tool for Valentina. Success feels like: “I have people waiting for me here, and when I go live, I can actually make money.”

**Journey summary:** Join → Set up → Go Live → Get discovered → Engage → Earn → Build regulars → Go Live again.

## Daniel, the social viewer

**Profile:** Daniel is 29, works full-time, and uses entertainment apps during breaks and in the evening. He uses TikTok, Instagram, YouTube, and live-streaming apps. He occasionally buys a small amount of coins when engaged.

**Main goal:** Relax, find entertainment, and interact with creators he likes.

**Motivations:** Live video feels more personal than prerecorded content. He enjoys creator interaction, being recognized when he enters, joking with other viewers, and discovering new personalities.

**Typical behavior:** He opens Discover without a particular creator in mind, tries several streams, stays when one catches his attention, follows creators he likes, and gradually becomes a regular. He may send a small gift after a creator acknowledges him or an entertaining moment.

**Pain points:** Boring or empty streams, repeated recommendations, difficulty finding someone interesting, aggressive gift requests, and feeling invisible in crowded streams.

**What he needs:** Fast discovery, useful stream previews and recommendations, Following notifications, simple chat, affordable gifts, reasons to return such as streaks or rewards, and recognition as a regular.

**Product lens:** Daniel opens Pulse VIP to see who is live and find something interesting happening now. He connects audience traffic to eventual monetization, but spending is not his reason to open the app.

**Journey summary:** Discover → Watch → Interact → Follow → Return → Become a regular → Gift.

## Marco, the VIP supporter

**Profile:** Marco is 38, an established professional or business owner with disposable income. He is busy during the day, uses Instagram, TikTok, and live or social apps, and tends to watch at night. He regularly buys coins and sends gifts.

**Main goal:** Enjoy entertainment while building a closer connection with the creators he supports.

**Motivations:** Be recognized, support favorite creators, have status in their communities, and access experiences that feel distinct from casual viewing.

**Typical behavior:** He follows fewer creators than Daniel but returns to them more often. He sends larger gifts, buys coins regularly, joins premium streams, and appreciates immediate recognition. He may boost a favorite creator to help her become more visible.

**Pain points:** Spending without appreciation, benefits that do not feel special, a confusing coin system, creators leaving the platform, and no visible distinction between a loyal supporter and a new visitor.

**What he needs:** Supporter status or badges, gifting history, loyalty or streak recognition, premium access, special gifts or animations, easy coin purchases, creator notifications, recognition on entry, and potentially benefits that grow with a creator relationship.

**Product lens:** Marco is seeking recognition, access, and participation. Coin purchases are a means to that experience.

**Journey summary:** Discover a creator → Follow → Become a regular → Gift → Build recognition → Gain supporter status → Access premium experiences → Deepen loyalty.

“VIP supporter” describes Marco's behavior and desired recognition. It does not automatically mean he owns the separate Pulse VIP subscription or has any particular subscription benefit. See [Pulse VIP](pulse-vip.md) for approved membership behavior and status.

## Valentina's creator journey

**Goal:** Build an audience, create loyal regulars, and generate consistent income.

| Stage | Action | Thought or emotion | Friction | Pulse VIP opportunity or feature idea |
| --- | --- | --- | --- | --- |
| 1. Invitation | Hears about Pulse VIP from a creator, agency, or the platform. | “Can I actually make money here?” Curious, but uncertain. | She does not know the platform. | Explain the creator value and earning model clearly. |
| 2. Signup | Creates an account, confirms she is 18 or older, and verifies her identity. | “Hopefully this doesn't take forever.” Impatient. | Verification may cause abandonment. | Keep onboarding fast and show progress. |
| 3. Creator setup | Adds a profile photo, cover, bio, country, and interests. | “I need my profile to look good.” Motivated. | Too many setup steps. | Show profile completeness and the most useful next step. |
| 4. First Live | Taps Go Live, adds a title and category, and starts streaming. | “Is anyone going to join?” Anxious. | Starting in an empty room. | Give new creators a path to discovery. |
| 5. First viewer | Notices someone enter and chat. | “Finally, someone!” Relieved. | The viewer may leave quickly. | Surface joins and offer conversation prompts. |
| 6. Engagement | Talks, responds to chat, and receives follows. | “They're actually staying.” Encouraged. | Hard to identify valuable viewers. | Highlight regulars, followers, and supporters. |
| 7. First gift | Receives a viewer's gift. | “I just earned something!” Excited. | Coin-to-earnings conversion is unclear. | Show the gift moment and understandable earnings. |
| 8. After Live | Ends the stream and reviews results. | “Was tonight worth my time?” Evaluative. | Weak analytics. | Summarize viewers, new followers, gifts, earnings, and top supporters. |
| 9. Building regulars | Streams consistently and greets returning viewers. | “I know these people now.” Connected. | Difficult to remember everyone. | Show regular-viewer indicators and supporter history. |
| 10. Monetization | Receives more gifts and offers premium experiences. | “I can actually build income here.” Hopeful. | Income may fluctuate. | Explore earnings goals, premium streams, and creator tools. |
| 11. Growth | Invites followers from other platforms. | “I want my people over here.” Ambitious. | Followers may resist installing another app. | Explore shareable invite links and referral incentives. |
| 12. Retention | Develops a recurring audience. | “My community is here.” Invested. | Competitors may offer better economics or tools. | Improve creator economics, recognition, and community tools. |

**Critical loop:** Go Live → Get discovered → Engage → Receive gifts → Recognize supporters → Build regulars → Go Live again.

**Early risk:** If her first few streams attract nobody, she may leave before monetization has a chance to matter. The “three streams” example is a product hypothesis to test, not an established retention threshold.

## Daniel's social viewer journey

**Goal:** Find entertaining people quickly, interact, and develop favorite creators.

| Stage | Action | Thought or emotion | Friction | Pulse VIP opportunity or feature idea |
| --- | --- | --- | --- | --- |
| 1. Invitation or discovery | Follows a creator or friend's invitation, or sees Pulse VIP elsewhere. | “What's on here?” Curious. | He does not know why he needs another app. | Take him directly to interesting live content. |
| 2. Signup | Creates an account and confirms he is 18 or older. | “Just let me see what's happening.” Impatient. | Too much onboarding before viewing. | Minimize steps before Discover. |
| 3. Discover | Scans the live creator grid. | “Who's interesting?” Browsing. | Streams look too similar. | Use useful thumbnails, categories, and popularity signals. |
| 4. Browsing | Enters and exits several Lives. | “No… no… maybe this one.” Selective. | Slow switching interrupts exploration. | Make loading and stream-to-stream navigation fast. |
| 5. Interesting Live | Finds a creator he likes. | “She's actually entertaining.” Engaged. | He does not know the creator yet. | Offer an easy profile preview while watching. |
| 6. Interaction | Sends a chat message. | “Let's see if she responds.” Hopeful. | His message may be missed. | Help creators notice new and active viewers. |
| 7. Recognition | Hears the creator say his username. | “She noticed me.” Delighted. | Recognition depends on the creator seeing him. | Make this human interaction easier; preserve its personal feel. |
| 8. Follow | Follows the creator. | “I'll watch her again.” Interested. | He may forget to return. | Notify him when followed creators go live. |
| 9. Return | Opens a notification for that creator's Live. | “Let me see what she's doing.” Anticipating. | Too many alerts become noise. | Make Live notifications relevant and personal. |
| 10. Regular | Returns often enough to be remembered. | “She remembers me.” Included. | His loyalty may be invisible. | Explore regular or streak recognition. |
| 11. First gift | Buys a small coin package and sends a gift. | “Let's see her reaction.” Excited. | Purchasing may interrupt the Live. | Keep the purchase and gift flow connected to the stream. |
| 12. Habit | Opens Pulse VIP even without an alert. | “Who's live?” Comfortable. | Discovery can become repetitive. | Personalize Discover while preserving variety. |

**Critical loop:** Open app → Discover → Find someone interesting → Chat → Get recognized → Follow → Return. Later: Return → Relationship → Gift.

**Product implication:** Give Daniel a reason to care about a creator before asking him to buy coins. The relationship gives a gift its meaning.

## Marco's VIP supporter journey

**Goal:** Build stronger relationships with favorite creators and receive recognition for his support.

| Stage | Action | Thought or emotion | Friction | Pulse VIP opportunity or feature idea |
| --- | --- | --- | --- | --- |
| 1. Discovery | Finds a creator through Discover. | “She's interesting.” Curious. | His first view feels like everyone else's. | Help him discover creators who fit his interests. |
| 2. Follow | Follows after watching. | “I'll come back.” Interested. | He may miss the next Live. | Offer relevant Live alerts. |
| 3. Repeat visits | Returns to the creator's Lives. | “I'm becoming a regular here.” Invested. | The creator may not remember him. | Show a returning-viewer indicator. |
| 4. First gift | Sends a gift. | “Let's see how she reacts.” Expectant. | The gift may feel insignificant. | Make the gift moment visible and satisfying. |
| 5. Recognition | Receives a personal thank-you. | “She knows who I am.” Appreciated. | Recognition may disappear next session. | Make supporter history useful to the creator. |
| 6. Increased spending | Buys larger coin packages. | “I'll support her tonight.” Intentional. | Payment friction. | Make coin purchasing simple. |
| 7. Status | Gains visible supporter recognition. | “People know I support this room.” Proud. | Status may feel cosmetic. | Give status meaningful, clearly defined benefits. |
| 8. Creator loyalty | Supports the same creator regularly. | “She's my favorite streamer.” Loyal. | The relationship has no visible history. | Explore creator-specific loyalty levels. |
| 9. Premium | Joins a premium experience. | “This feels more exclusive.” Valued. | Premium may feel too similar to ordinary viewing. | Make the experience distinctly valuable. |
| 10. Boosting | Boosts a favorite creator's visibility. | “I'm helping her grow.” Effective. | Gifts may be his only support option. | Explore boosts as another support mechanism. |
| 11. Social status | Becomes known to other viewers. | “I'm part of this community.” Included. | Support can feel invisible. | Explore badges, entrance treatment, or leaderboards. |
| 12. Long-term loyalty | Maintains the supporter relationship. | “Pulse is where my creators are.” Committed. | His favorite creator may leave. | Retain creators with strong audience and income tools. |

**Critical loop:** Return → Recognition → Gift → Creator reaction → Status → Exclusive access → Stronger connection → Return.

**Product implication:** Marco values recognition, status, access, and impact. Those outcomes should guide supporter features rather than coin sales alone.

## How the journeys connect

Daniel discovers Valentina, interacts, follows, and returns. Valentina recognizes him, helping him become a regular. He may begin gifting; some viewers with similar paths may eventually behave more like Marco. Marco's support can increase Valentina's income, giving her a reason to stream more often. Her streams then give more viewers something to discover.

**Shared growth loop:** Creators → Live content → Viewers → Relationships → Gifts and premium participation → Creator earnings → More creator activity and content.

This loop is a proposed framework for evaluating the product roadmap. The shift from Daniel-like behavior to Marco-like behavior is a possible path, not an expectation that every viewer will spend heavily. When prioritizing a feature, consider both its direct benefit to one persona and whether it strengthens a connection between their journeys.

## Compare a feature against the personas

| Persona | Primary job | A feature helps when it... |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Valentina | Grow a returning audience and earn reliably | Brings suitable viewers in, helps her keep them engaged, supports safe interactions, or makes earnings understandable. |
| Daniel | Find an interesting live experience quickly | Helps him discover, engage, follow, and return without requiring spending. |
| Marco | Support a creator and feel recognized | Makes support simple and meaningful through appreciation, status, access, or participation. |

For each proposed feature, record **who it serves**, **which step of that person's journey it improves**, **the problem it solves**, and **how success will be observed**. A feature can serve more than one persona, but the benefit for each should be stated separately. Treat items in “What they need” as opportunities to assess, not automatic scope or a promise of current functionality.
